# Project Northstar: Five-Page Field Readiness Brief

## Page 1: Purpose, Schedule, and Ownership

Project Northstar is a six-week digital-access pilot at Harborview Community Center, and it was designed to help 240 families set up safe devices. The pilot begins April 14, 2027, the public workshop will happen on May 18, 2027, and final reports are due June 2, 2027. Although the steering committee approved a budget of $18,750, it has not approved overtime, catering, or any paid advertising. This distinction matters because several volunteers have already asked if extra hours will be reimbursed.

Mira Chen, who leads operations, said, "We can move quickly, but we cannot skip consent." Her reminder was aimed at everybody on the team, especially Jonah Patel and me. Each coordinator needs to confirm their assignment before Friday, and neither the trainers nor the site manager is allowed to assume somebody else completed it. If workers are unsure, they should email mira.chen@example.org; replies get archived in the project record.

On April 30, 2027, Jonah Patel will deliver 240 setup kits to Room B-214 by 9:30 a.m. Elena Ruiz will inspect the shipment, count the security seals, and log any damage in the inventory dashboard. Only Mira Chen, Jonah Patel, and Elena Ruiz may access that dashboard; no volunteer may publish screenshots. This restriction follows the privacy plan and needs to be clarified before orientation.

Dr. Soren Bell must finish the accessibility review by May 6, 2027. The review covers large text, keyboard navigation, captions, and whether the instructions can be understood by a reader with limited technical experience. After Dr. Bell read the draft instructions, he found the color contrast inadequate. Dr. Bell should also check that every diagram has alt text, every video includes captions, and every QR code works on both Android and iOS.

The team has three immediate tasks:

- Jonah: deliver the kits, label every carton, and update the count.
- Elena: verify seals, photograph damage, and submit the log by 4:00 p.m.
- Mira: send the consent script to ops@northstar.example.org, train 14 volunteers, and confirm Room B-214.

## Page 2: Data, Safety, and Communication Rules

No student records may leave Harborview Community Center, even if a parent requests them by email. Volunteers may not photograph minors, upload sign-in sheets, or share access codes. The three loaner tablets must use full-device encryption and lock automatically after 5 minutes. Before the tablets are issued, Jonah will configure them, and Elena will verify the settings on May 12, 2027. That sequence is mandatory; it cannot be reversed just because the line becomes long.

Participants can read the current brief at https://northstar.example.org/brief?rev=4, and they may submit attendance at https://northstar.example.org/checkin. The first link is public; the second link requires a six-digit access code. Do not copy either address into social media posts, and no one is permitted to create a shorter link. The communications lead asked Mira to post an announcement herself, which she declined because all public posting requires written approval from the steering committee.

During orientation, trainers must explain what data is collected, why it is needed, and how long we keep it. They also need to show participants where to request deletion because trust matters. If someone refuses consent, staff must still answer general questions but must not enroll them or save their details. A parent may withdraw later; their kit remains theirs, yet their account data must be deleted within 10 business days.

The privacy checklist contains seven controls, but only five have assigned owners. Mira asked the team to review it carefully and mark incomplete items in red; however, nobody was sure who should approve the final version. The checklist is not optional. Its purpose is to prevent staff from collecting information they do not need, and it also reminds volunteers that a helpful intention does not cancel a participant's rights.

## Page 3: Risks, Decisions, and Definition of Done

The main risks are late shipment, confusing consent, device loss, and instructions that assume everybody owns a smartphone. The backup plan includes 12 printed guides and a staffed help desk, neither of which is ready. Mira asked Jonah whether the shipment was insured. He replied that the carrier confirmed coverage last Tuesday, but he did not attach evidence. That answer needs to be verified before April 30, 2027.

There is also disagreement about check-in. Elena favors one shared tablet because it is simpler; Jonah prefers paper because the network sometimes fails. A shared tablet creates a queue, while paper forms create another privacy risk. The steering committee must choose one method by May 6, 2027; otherwise, Mira will use encrypted offline check-in on the three loaner tablets. This fallback may be used only for attendance; volunteers must not add medical notes, family income, immigration status, or unrelated comments.

Northstar is ready when all kits are counted, every volunteer has completed training, accessibility issues are closed, consent language is approved, and the check-in method works offline. Mira will run a rehearsal on May 12, 2027, record defects, and then assign owners. Owners must correct critical defects by May 18, 2027; minor wording issues are due by June 2, 2027. The team should be careful, transparent, and diligent in documenting decisions. If these conditions are met, the pilot can begin responsibly without changing its scope or its $18,750 budget.

The steering committee also wants a status summary every Monday, but it has not agreed on the format. Jonah suggested a spreadsheet that lists every task, owner, and deadline. Elena said the number of columns was too large for a phone screen. Mira responded that less information would make the report harder to audit. The group needs a format that is readable and complete and does not expose private participant data.

Mira wants all 14 volunteers to finish every module in order. Yet Jonah told one group that they could skip Module 1 if they had volunteered last year. ## Page 5: Incident Response, Measurement, and Closeout

Any lost tablet, exposed access code, or accidental photograph of a minor is a reportable incident. The person who discovers one must notify Mira Chen within 30 minutes and preserve the device without deleting anything. Mira then contacts Harborview's privacy officer, David Kim, and records only the facts that are known. Staff should not guess who caused the problem or promise an outcome before the review is complete.

Critical incidents must be assessed within 4 hours. A written incident summary is due within 24 hours, even when the investigation remains open. The summary must list the time discovered, device identifier, people notified, containment steps, and any unanswered questions. It must not include medical information or immigration status. David Kim decides whether parents need to be contacted; volunteers do not make that decision themselves.

Project results will be measured with four approved numbers: kits issued, completed workshops, support tickets resolved, and deletion requests completed within 10 business days. The team may not add a satisfaction score unless the steering committee approves the survey first. Elena will export the approved totals on June 2, 2027, remove names, and send the file to Mira. Jonah will review the kit count, Priya will verify the ticket count, and David Kim will confirm deletion timing.

At closeout, Mira must compare spending against the $18,750 pilot budget and the separate $4,200 Lexford Digital cap. Unused money does not authorize new purchases. Receipts are stored locally for seven years, while participant account data are deleted according to the consent schedule. These records have different purposes and should not be combined just because both relate to the same project.

The final review asks whether Northstar met its scope safely, not whether every participant liked every instruction. If a target was missed, the report should say so plainly, explain the verified reason, and name the corrective owner. The committee wants honesty but also wants the report to sound confident. An accurate closeout is more useful than a polished document that hides problems. When all required reports are approved, Mira archives the project record, revokes temporary access, and confirms that no participant text remains in the help-desk queue.
